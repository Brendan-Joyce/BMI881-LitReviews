# 12/9 ~ Leek ~ Tackling the widespread and critical impact of batch effects in high-throughput data
## Literature Review

  Leek’s work in “Tackling the widespread and critical impact of batch effects in high-throughput data” shines a light on the importance of considering potential batch effects in conducting biological experimental design and data analysis. To demonstrate the risks that batch effects play in hallucinating biologically relevant signals, they explore a variety of different data sets, such as one released by a recent bladder cancer study, to identify spurious correlations between biological signal shifts and surrogate measurements of batch like day of sample processing or batch labels. These surrogate measurements don’t suggest the direct relationship between time and sample signal but rather are used to represent details such as personnel differences during sample processing or environment shifts like weather or ozone accumulation. This paper does not explore a breadth of methods for correcting batch effects, outside of calling lip service to methods like quantile normalization, instead they focus on the importance of recognizing batch effects and correcting it prior to publishing one’s analyses. 

  This paper reads as a reminder to consider the risks implicit to experimental heterogeneity rather than an outright review. To its credit, I think this serves the paper very well. It creates a very clean readable narrative for a highly important topic in both experimental and analytical design. Additionally, their focus on appealing to biologists and computational experts with their respective analyses and recommendations gives their work far greater reach. The only issue I have with the paper is that it was something that needed to be said at all. Since middle school, details like batch effect in experimental design were concepts we were introduced to. So, for some much of the scientific community to be making these mistakes that it warranted this type of meta-analysis in so late as 2010, I was caught a bit off guard. With all that said, the paper’s subject matter is reported in an approachable and calls out clear simple interventions we can take to improve the quality of our science going forward.
